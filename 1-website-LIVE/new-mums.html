The research behind it

It isn't the noise that wakes you. It's the jump.

Sleep lab · ICU noise

Same hospital noise. A steady sound underneath it. Far fewer wake-ups.

Researchers played recorded intensive-care noise to sleeping volunteers. Brief awakenings rose from 13 an hour to 48. With steady white noise added under the same ICU noise, they came back down to 16. What decided whether someone woke wasn't how loud the peaks were. It was how far they jumped above the background.

Hospital shift workers

Tested on people sleeping at odd hours.

26 emergency-medicine doctors working overnight shifts used noise-masking earbuds for two weeks. On the days they used them, they reported better sleep quality and less sleepiness and tension than in the two weeks before. The changes were modest and self-reported, but consistent.

Duggan et al., JMIR Formative Research, 2022. Read the abstract
These studies tested plain white noise and masking earbuds, not Orvel, and they're small. We show them because they explain the principle every Orvel track is built on: a steady floor makes the same noise a smaller jump. Orvel is wellness audio, not a medical treatment.
