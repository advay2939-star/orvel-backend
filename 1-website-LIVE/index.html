We don't just ask what sounds you like.
We ask what keeps you awake.

A detailed brief after checkout — around twenty questions, five to seven minutes. Every answer changes a real production decision — not a label on a preset.

Built for the window you actually have — a track for a 6-hour daytime sleep isn't shaped like one for a 20-minute reset.

Where the evidence sits

Covering the noise is the part with real evidence behind it.

The most reliable thing sound does for sleep is unglamorous: it masks. A continuous, even bed of sound raises the floor of your room so that the door downstairs, the road outside and the neighbour's television stop arriving as separate events your brain has to evaluate. That's the mechanism we lean on hardest, and it's the one with the strongest support.

Binaural beats are the more interesting, less settled part. Play two slightly different pure tones — one into each ear — and you perceive a third, slow pulse that isn't present in either signal. Research has explored whether this supports relaxation, anxiety and sleep. Results vary, and the effect sizes reported are modest. We include a binaural layer where your playback setup makes it meaningful, and we don't build the product's promise on it.

What we won't tell you is that your brainwaves automatically synchronise to a magic number. The honest version: a well-designed soundscape, paced deliberately, mixed for your actual environment, gives your attention less to do. That's worth building carefully. It isn't worth exaggerating.
